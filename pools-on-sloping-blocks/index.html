Building a pool on a sloping block in Wollongong

In short: On land Council knows or suspects is subject to slope instability, Wollongong DCP Chapter E12 generally requires a geotechnical impact assessment report with a development application, signed off on Council's Form M11. Chapter B1 also asks for a pool to sit as close to existing ground level as possible, which on a slope can mean excavating the high side, and limits retaining wall heights.

Many Wollongong backyards climb towards the escarpment. This guide summarises the Council and Sydney Water rules that affect pools on sloping land, with links to the source documents. It is not engineering or planning advice.

Last updated: 27 November 2026

This page summarises Wollongong Development Control Plan 2009 (Chapters E12 and B1) and Sydney Water's building plan approval rules so you know what to raise with your builder and certifier. Your builder, a certifier, a geotechnical engineer or Wollongong City Council can confirm what applies to your lot.

Chapter E12: when a geotechnical report is needed

Chapter E12 of the Wollongong DCP, Geotechnical Assessment of Slope Instability (in force from 19 July 2021), applies to land in the City of Wollongong that is known or suspected to be subject to slope instability. Council says this mostly shows up as rockfall, landslides or debris flows on hillside land, especially after prolonged or intense rain, and also along the coast.

Council decides whether your site may be affected using its property database and maps, site inspections, existing geotechnical reports and other information. If Council is satisfied the site has an acceptable risk, the application can go ahead without a report. Otherwise, the chapter applies to the majority of development on that land.

The chapter lists some work it does not apply to, including exempt development, complying development as defined in the Wollongong LEP, minor earthworks or landscaping with cut or fill no more than 600 mm high, and certain articulated timber or steel decks. A pool excavation is usually deeper than 600 mm, so if your lot is flagged and the pool goes through a development application, expect to need a report.

What the report has to cover

For a development application, Chapter E12 asks for a geotechnical impact assessment report that includes, among other things:

  • a review of any history of slope instability on the site or nearby land
  • survey-based site and cross-section plans with existing and proposed contours, showing stormwater, sewer and water pipes, trees and other hazards
  • a site inspection in every case, plus investigation such as boreholes or test pits where needed
  • a risk assessment under the Australian Geomechanics Society's 2007 landslide risk management guidelines
  • a site classification under AS 2870 (residential slabs and footings)
  • a clear statement on whether the site is suitable, with recommendations on footings, earthworks, surface and subsurface drainage, structural systems, ongoing maintenance and the inspections needed during construction.

The report must come with Council's Form M11, signed by the geotechnical engineer or engineering geologist who prepared or verified it. Reports more than two years old, or prepared for an earlier subdivision, aren't accepted unless they're reviewed and re-certified on Form M11. The structural design must also be given to the principal certifier before work starts, with Form M12 from the structural engineer.

Chapter B1: how the pool sits on the slope

For a pool assessed under the DCP, Chapter B1 (Residential Development) says:

  • An in-ground pool should be built with its top edge as close as possible to existing ground level. On sloping sites this may mean excavating on the high side so the pool sticks out of the ground as little as possible on the low side.
  • The water line must be at least 900 mm from any side or rear boundary.
  • Decking around the pool must be no more than 600 mm above existing ground. Coping must be no more than 1.4 m above existing ground, and no more than 300 mm wide where it's more than 600 mm above ground.
  • The pool can't go over an easement or within the zone of influence of a public sewer main or drainage pipe, and the excavation must not clash with any stormwater line, including inter-allotment drainage, which must be located and shown on the site plan first.
  • Overflow paths must be provided and must not direct water onto the neighbour's property.
  • The pump and filter should be as far as practicable from neighbouring homes and enclosed in an acoustic enclosure.

Retaining walls around the pool

Terracing a sloping yard for a pool usually means retaining walls. If a wall needs a development application, Chapter B1 generally limits it to 600 mm high within 900 mm of a side or rear boundary, or 1 m if set back further, requires terracing with no more than 1 m of vertical rise per step and a 1 m horizontal setback between steps, and requires any wall over 1 m to be designed by an engineer. In areas of known or suspected slope instability, Council may also ask for a geotechnical and structural engineer's report on the wall. Drainage behind walls must be disposed of in a way Council approves and must not discharge onto neighbouring land.

Sydney Water approval

Sydney Water says you need its building plan approval before work starts for projects including pools, retaining walls, landscaping and excavation, because the work may be near its pipes. You apply through Sydney Water Tap in. If your plans don't affect its assets you get approval on screen; if they do, you're referred to a water servicing coordinator, and some cases need a more detailed review.

Questions to ask a pool builder on a sloping block

  • Is my lot flagged for slope instability on Council's records, and will the pool need a development application or can it go through complying development?
  • Who will arrange the geotechnical report and Form M11, and is the cost in the quote?
  • How much cut, fill and retaining is involved, and who designs the retaining walls?
  • Where will surface water, overflow and subsurface drainage go?
  • Have you checked sewer and stormwater lines and lodged with Sydney Water Tap in?
  • How will excavated soil and rock be removed from the site, and is that priced?
